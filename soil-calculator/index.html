How much soil do I need?

Topsoil is normally ordered by volume, so the key measurements are the surface area and the depth of the new soil layer. Multiply length by width to find area, then multiply by depth to find volume.

This calculator also estimates bulk weight and the number of bags required. Bag count is useful for smaller garden projects, while cubic metres or cubic yards are more practical for bulk deliveries.

Choosing a soil depth

The right depth depends on the project. A thin lawn top-dressing uses far less material than filling a new raised bed. New planting areas may require a deeper layer so roots have enough usable soil, while established lawns may only need a shallow dressing.

Always measure the actual depth you intend to add. Existing soil level, drainage and settling can change the final quantity.

Soil calculation example

A bed measuring 6 m × 3 m with 10 cm of new soil needs 1.8 m³ before allowance. Adding 5% gives about 1.89 m³.

Bulk soil versus bags

Bagged soil can be convenient when access is difficult or the project is small. Bulk soil is usually easier for larger areas. The bag estimate here simply divides total litres by the bag size you enter, then rounds up to a whole bag.

Frequently asked questions

Does soil compact after installation?

Yes. Loose material can settle after watering and foot traffic, which is why a small allowance can be useful.

How much does topsoil weigh?

Weight varies with moisture and composition. The calculator uses approximate densities for planning only.

Can I calculate raised-bed soil?

Yes. Use the inside length and width of the bed and the depth you plan to fill.
