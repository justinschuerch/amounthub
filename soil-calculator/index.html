How much soil do I need?

Topsoil is normally ordered by volume, so the key measurements are the surface area and the depth of the new soil layer. Multiply length by width to find area, then multiply by depth to find volume. The calculator can also estimate approximate bulk weight and the number of bags required.

For smaller garden projects, bags may be convenient because they are easy to carry and store. For lawns, large beds and major landscaping jobs, cubic metres or cubic yards are usually more useful because bulk delivery quickly becomes more practical than handling dozens of bags.

Choosing the right soil depth

The right depth depends heavily on what you are trying to achieve. A light lawn top-dressing uses far less material than building a new raised bed. A planting area for shallow-rooted annuals may need less added soil than a deep vegetable bed. Existing soil quality, compaction and drainage also influence whether adding a thin layer is enough.

Soil calculation example

A bed measuring 6 m × 3 m with 10 cm of new soil needs 1.8 m³ before allowance. Adding 5% gives about 1.89 m³. At an assumed density of 1.30 t/m³, that is roughly 2.46 tonnes. With 40-litre bags, the same volume would require about 48 bags.

Why topsoil weight varies

Soil is a mixture of mineral particles, organic matter, water and air. A dry, loose, compost-rich blend can be much lighter than dense moist topsoil. That means there is no single perfect conversion from cubic metres to tonnes. The density selector is intended to provide a planning range, not a certified transport weight.

If you are arranging delivery, especially where vehicle access or load limits matter, use the supplier's quoted weight or bulk density. Their product may differ significantly from a generic topsoil assumption.

Bulk soil versus bagged soil

Bagged soil is useful for balconies, small beds, awkward access and projects where only a few hundred litres are needed. Bulk soil is usually easier for larger quantities, but it requires somewhere for the delivery to be tipped and a way to move the material around the site.

The bag estimate on this page simply divides total litres by the bag size you enter and rounds up to a complete bag. Packaging varies, so enter the exact litre size printed on the product rather than relying on a standard bag assumption.

How settling changes the final level

Freshly placed soil often sits higher and looser than it will after watering, rainfall and normal foot traffic. Fine particles move into gaps and the material consolidates. This is one reason a small extra allowance can be useful. It also explains why filling a raised bed exactly flush with the top on day one may leave the finished level lower later.

However, more is not always better. Overfilling around trunks, stems, walls or drainage details can create problems. The calculator helps estimate quantity, but final levels should still suit the planting and site design.

Measuring irregular garden beds

For L-shaped or curved beds, split the space into smaller rectangles and calculate each separately. Curved areas can often be approximated by a few simple shapes. If one half of a bed needs 5 cm of soil and the other half needs 15 cm, calculate the two areas separately instead of using one depth for the entire site.

This method takes a little longer, but it is usually more accurate than measuring one overall rectangle that includes paths, edging or areas that will not receive soil.

What type of soil should I order?

“Topsoil” can describe many different products. Screened topsoil, planting mixes, lawn soils, vegetable blends and compost-enriched soils can have different textures and uses. Quantity is only one part of the purchase decision. Check whether the product is suitable for turf, vegetables, ornamental planting or filling, and ask about drainage and organic content if those properties matter to the project.

Frequently asked questions

Does soil compact after installation?

Yes. Loose material can settle after watering, rainfall and traffic, so a modest allowance can be useful.

How much does topsoil weigh?

Weight varies with moisture, texture and organic content. The calculator uses approximate densities for planning only.

Can I calculate raised-bed soil?

Yes. Use the inside length and width of the bed and the depth you plan to fill. Subtract any existing fill if the bed is not empty.

Can I use the calculator for a lawn?

Yes. Enter the lawn dimensions and the thickness of topsoil or dressing you actually plan to add.

Should I order extra soil?

A small allowance can cover settling and uneven ground. The default is 5%, but the right amount depends on how accurately the site has been measured.

Why does the bag count round up?

You normally cannot buy a fraction of a sealed bag, so the calculator rounds to the next whole bag.
