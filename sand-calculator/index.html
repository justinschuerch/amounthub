How much sand do I need?

Sand quantities are usually based on the volume of the space you want to fill. Measure the length and width of the area, then choose the planned sand depth. The calculator converts those dimensions to one common unit before multiplying them together.

For a rectangular area, the basic formula is length × width × depth = sand volume. A weight estimate is then calculated using the selected sand density. Volume is normally the better figure for comparing bulk deliveries, while weight can be useful when a supplier sells by the tonne or ton.

Sand volume, weight and density

A cubic metre of sand does not have one fixed weight. Fine grains pack differently from coarse grains, and damp sand can be heavier because water fills the voids between particles. Loose material may also occupy more space before it settles or is compacted. That is why this calculator offers several density assumptions instead of presenting one number as exact.

If your supplier lists a bulk density for the exact product you plan to buy, their figure should take priority. The calculator's density choices are best used for early planning, comparing delivery sizes and checking whether an order is in the right range.

Sand calculation example

A paving bed that is 5 m long, 3 m wide and 5 cm deep has a theoretical volume of 0.75 m³. With 10% extra, the planning quantity becomes about 0.83 m³. If the selected density is 1.60 t/m³, that volume corresponds to roughly 1.32 tonnes.

The allowance is applied to volume before the weight is calculated. That keeps the result consistent: if you increase the quantity of sand you plan to buy, the estimated weight rises by the same proportion.

How to measure irregular areas

Not every sand project is a clean rectangle. For an L-shaped patio or garden area, split the shape into two or more rectangles, calculate each part separately and add the volumes. For a long path that changes width, divide it into shorter sections and use an average width for each section. This approach is usually more reliable than trying to force one set of dimensions to represent the whole job.

If the depth varies, take several depth measurements and use a reasonable average. Large low spots can use much more material than a simple surface-area estimate suggests, so correcting the base before ordering can make the estimate more predictable.

Loose sand versus compacted sand

Sand can settle when it is watered, vibrated or walked on. A supplier may quote a delivered loose volume, while your project specification may describe a finished compacted layer. Those are not always identical. If the finished depth is critical, use the compacted depth you need and ask the supplier or contractor whether an additional compaction factor should be included.

This is especially important below pavers, slabs and other surfaces where final levels matter. For a casual sandbox or decorative use, compaction may be much less important.

Choosing the right kind of sand

Different sands are intended for different jobs. Bedding sand, concrete sand, masonry sand, play sand and fine decorative sand can vary in particle size, cleanliness and grading. The calculator estimates quantity; it does not decide which material is suitable. For structural or drainage-sensitive work, follow the design or product specification.

Using the correct material can matter more than achieving a perfectly precise quantity. A small surplus of the right sand is usually more useful than an exact volume of the wrong grade.

Ordering sand without running short

A modest extra allowance can cover measurement error, uneven excavation, spillage and settlement. The default is 10%, but a simple, well-measured rectangular area may need less, while irregular shapes or uncertain depths may justify more. Avoid automatically adding a very large percentage because unused bulk sand can be difficult to store or remove.

Before ordering, compare the calculator result with the supplier's minimum load, bag size or delivery increment. Suppliers may round bulk orders to convenient quantities, so the final purchase amount can differ slightly from the mathematical estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should paving sand be?

The required depth depends on the paving system and local installation guidance. Measure the finished compacted layer specified for the project rather than guessing from the loose material pile.

Does the calculator include waste?

Yes. The default allowance is 10%, and you can change it before calculating to suit the shape and uncertainty of the project.

Can I use feet and inches?

Yes. Switch to Imperial to enter feet or inches and see volume in cubic yards and approximate weight in US tons and pounds.

Is wet sand heavier than dry sand?

Usually, yes. Moisture adds mass and can also change how the grains pack. For an exact order, use the density or conversion supplied by your vendor.

Can this estimate sand for a sandbox?

Yes. Measure the inside length and width, then enter the desired fill depth. Remember to leave free space at the top rather than filling the box completely.

Should I order by volume or weight?

Use the unit your supplier sells in. The calculator shows both so you can compare a volume-based plan with a weight-based quote.
