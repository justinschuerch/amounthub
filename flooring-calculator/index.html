Exact area versus what you need to buy

A room may mathematically need 22.1 m² of flooring, but flooring is sold in complete packs. The useful purchase quantity is therefore the first whole pack count that covers the room plus waste. AmountHub shows both the adjusted area and the package count so the rounding is transparent.

Worked example

A 5 m × 4 m room covers 20 m². With 10% waste, the planned requirement is 22 m². If a pack covers 2.2 m², the project requires 10 packs. If you deliberately keep one unopened spare pack, buy 11.

Why pack coverage matters more than plank dimensions

Manufacturers already know how many planks are in a pack and what area they cover. For purchasing, the stated pack coverage avoids unnecessary plank-by-plank arithmetic. Individual plank dimensions are still useful for layout decisions, but pack coverage is usually the cleaner quantity input.

Irregular rooms and fixed areas

Split L-shaped rooms into rectangles and add their areas. Include closets and alcoves where the same floor continues. Exclude only permanent areas that genuinely will not receive flooring, and follow the installation instructions for floating floors around fixed cabinets and heavy built-ins.

Spare material can be valuable

Product lines are discontinued, colour batches change and installed flooring ages. Keeping a small amount from the original batch can make a future repair much easier. The planning options let you deliberately add one unopened spare pack instead of hiding that decision inside a larger waste percentage.

Before ordering

  • Confirm pack coverage from the exact product.
  • Choose waste based on layout complexity.
  • Check installation rules around cabinets, expansion gaps and transitions.
  • Consider one spare pack for future repairs.
  • Keep underlay, trims and levelling products as separate estimates.
