How much flooring do I need?

Start with the floor area: multiply room length by room width. Flooring is commonly purchased by coverage rather than by individual plank count, so the calculator adds your chosen waste allowance to the room area and compares that total with the coverage printed on each pack.

Because flooring is sold in complete packs, the final pack count is rounded up. The area result shows how much material the project needs before packaging forces that final rounding.

Flooring calculation example

A 5 m × 4 m room covers 20 m². With 10% waste, the planned flooring quantity is 22 m². If each pack covers 2.2 m², the estimate is exactly 10 packs.

If the pack coverage were 2.0 m² instead, the same room would require 11 packs. Always use the coverage from the actual product label.

Laminate, vinyl and engineered wood

The same area-based formula works for many packaged floor coverings, including click laminate, luxury vinyl plank, engineered timber and some cork products. The key is that the packaging states how much area one unopened pack covers.

Solid timber sold by board foot, carpet sold by roll width, sheet vinyl and ceramic tile can require different estimating methods. For tile, use the dedicated Tile Calculator.

Measuring L-shaped and irregular rooms

Divide the floor into simple rectangles. Measure each rectangle, calculate its area and add the results before applying the waste allowance. This works well for rooms with alcoves, hallways, bay windows and open-plan areas that change width.

Do not include permanent areas that will definitely remain uncovered, such as a fixed masonry hearth or built-in platform, unless the flooring will continue beneath them.

Should flooring run under cabinets?

The answer depends on the flooring system and installation instructions. Floating floors often need expansion space and may not be intended to run beneath fixed cabinets, while some glued or nailed systems can be installed differently. The calculator cannot decide that detail, so measure only the area the chosen installation method will actually cover.

Why pack coverage matters more than plank size

Two laminate products can have different plank dimensions yet cover nearly the same area per box. For purchasing, pack coverage is the simplest reliable input because it already accounts for the number and size of planks inside the package. Individual plank dimensions are more useful when planning row layout and visible joints.

Keeping spare boards

Leftover flooring can be valuable for future repairs. Product lines are discontinued, colour batches change and sunlight can alter installed material over time. If a damaged board needs replacing years later, having a few original planks can make the repair easier.

For a distinctive or expensive floor, you may intentionally order an extra pack beyond the mathematical installation requirement. That is a personal planning choice rather than part of the default calculation.

What else does a flooring project need?

The calculator covers finish flooring only. Depending on the product, the project may also require underlay, vapour barrier, adhesive, levelling compound, trims, thresholds, expansion profiles and skirting. Those materials are measured differently and should not simply be included in the flooring pack count.

Subfloor condition and quantity

An uneven subfloor does not directly change room area, but it can create more waste if planks are damaged during fitting or if the installer must alter the layout. Correcting high and low spots before installation can improve both the finished floor and the predictability of the material estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for laminate flooring?

Yes. Enter the coverage per pack shown on the laminate packaging.

What about irregular rooms?

Split the room into rectangles, add their areas and then apply the waste allowance.

Does it work for vinyl planks?

Yes, as long as the product has a known area coverage per pack.

Should I include closets?

Include any closet or alcove where the same flooring will be installed.

Why does the calculator round packs up?

Retailers sell complete packs, so even a small fraction beyond a full pack requires another one.

Can I use it for carpet?

Not reliably. Carpet rolls involve roll width, seam placement and direction, so a dedicated carpet estimator is better.
