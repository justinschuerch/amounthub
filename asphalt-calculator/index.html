How much asphalt do I need?

Asphalt is commonly ordered by weight, but the starting point is volume. Measure the pavement length and width, choose the intended compacted thickness, then multiply those values to find the compacted volume.

The calculator multiplies volume by asphalt density to estimate tonnes. The default density is 2.4 tonnes per cubic metre, a useful planning value, but the actual mix supplied to your project may differ.

Compacted depth matters

The depth entered here should represent the finished compacted asphalt layer. Loose asphalt is thicker before rolling, so using uncompacted delivery depth can overstate the final paving thickness.

Driveways, footpaths, parking areas and road surfaces can require different pavement structures. This calculator estimates only the asphalt layer and does not design the aggregate base underneath it.

Asphalt calculation example

A rectangular area measuring 10 m × 4 m at a compacted depth of 5 cm has a volume of 2.0 m³. At 2.4 t/m³, that is about 4.8 tonnes before adding an allowance.

Why add an allowance?

Edges, level corrections, small measurement errors and material left in the truck or paver can all affect the final quantity. A modest allowance can help avoid running short, but too much surplus can be expensive because hot-mix asphalt has limited reuse once delivered.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate a driveway?

Yes. Enter the driveway dimensions and the compacted asphalt thickness specified for the project.

Does this include the gravel base?

No. The result is for asphalt only. Use the Gravel Calculator separately for an aggregate base estimate.

Why is supplier density important?

Different asphalt mixes have different densities. A supplier-provided mix density will produce a more accurate weight estimate.
