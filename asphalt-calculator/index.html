How much asphalt do I need?

Asphalt is commonly ordered by weight, but the calculation begins with volume. Measure the length and width of the area, choose the intended compacted thickness, and multiply those values to find compacted volume. The calculator then multiplies that volume by asphalt density to estimate tonnes or US tons.

The default density of 2.4 tonnes per cubic metre is a useful planning value for many dense asphalt mixes, but it is not universal. If your supplier gives a density or a conversion from square area and thickness to weight, use their value for the final order.

Compacted depth versus loose depth

The depth entered in this calculator should represent the finished compacted asphalt layer. Hot mix is placed loose and then compressed by rolling. The loose layer can be noticeably thicker than the finished layer, so using loose depth as though it were compacted depth can distort the estimate.

For a contractor-designed pavement, use the layer thickness shown on the specification. Driveways, footpaths, parking bays and road surfaces can all use different layer structures depending on traffic, climate and base conditions.

Asphalt calculation example

A rectangular area measuring 10 m × 4 m at a compacted depth of 5 cm has a volume of 2.0 m³. At 2.4 t/m³, that equals about 4.8 tonnes before allowance. Adding 5% gives an estimated order quantity of about 5.04 tonnes.

Why asphalt density changes

Different mixes contain different aggregate sizes, binder contents and void structures. Compaction level also matters. Two mixes covering the same area at the same thickness can therefore have different weights. Reclaimed asphalt content, aggregate type and specification can all influence the density used for ordering.

This calculator keeps density editable so the result can match a supplier or project specification rather than forcing one generic conversion onto every job.

Why add an allowance?

Even a carefully measured project can need slightly more material than the pure geometric volume. Edges, surface corrections, small changes in depth, material left in the truck or paver and handwork around transitions can all add demand. A modest allowance helps reduce the risk of running short.

At the same time, hot-mix asphalt is time-sensitive and excess material can be expensive to waste. For a large order, discuss the calculated quantity with the plant or paving contractor rather than automatically adding a large percentage.

Asphalt and the aggregate base are separate

The asphalt result covers the asphalt layer only. Many pavements also require a compacted aggregate base or sub-base underneath. That base can be much thicker than the asphalt itself and uses a different material density. Use the Gravel Calculator for a separate aggregate estimate if your project includes a stone base.

Keeping these layers separate makes the estimate clearer and avoids treating the entire pavement depth as asphalt.

How to measure a driveway that changes width

If the driveway is wider near the road, garage or parking area, divide it into sections. Measure each rectangle separately and add the areas. Curved sections can be approximated with several shorter rectangles. For an irregular repair, measure the longest and shortest dimensions and use multiple smaller zones rather than one oversized bounding rectangle.

Ordering considerations

Asphalt plants may have minimum order quantities, delivery charges and limits on how long material can remain workable. A small job can therefore be governed as much by logistics as by the theoretical weight. Access for the delivery truck, paving equipment and compaction equipment should be considered before ordering.

The calculator is intended for quantity planning and comparison. It does not replace a pavement design, structural specification or contractor's assessment of base preparation and drainage.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate a driveway?

Yes. Enter the driveway dimensions and the compacted asphalt thickness specified for the project.

Does this include the gravel base?

No. The result is for asphalt only. Calculate the aggregate base separately.

Why is supplier density important?

Different asphalt mixes have different compacted densities, so supplier data can improve the weight estimate.

Should I enter compacted or loose thickness?

Use the finished compacted thickness. The loose layer before rolling is not the same quantity basis.

Can I use imperial units?

Yes. Imperial mode accepts feet and inches and returns approximate US tons, pounds and cubic yards.

Is 5% extra always enough?

No single allowance is right for every job. Simple areas may need little extra, while repairs and irregular paving can be less predictable.
