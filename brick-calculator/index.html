How many bricks do I need?

Brick estimating begins with wall face area. Multiply wall length by wall height, then divide that area by the effective face area of one brick including its mortar joint. The calculator adds a waste allowance and rounds up to a whole brick.

This method estimates a single visible leaf of brickwork. Cavity walls, solid multi-leaf walls, piers, returns and decorative details can require more bricks than a simple flat wall of the same face area.

Brick calculation example

A wall 5 m long and 2.4 m high has an area of 12 m². With a 21.5 cm × 6.5 cm brick and 1 cm mortar joints, the calculator estimates the number of brick-and-joint modules that fit in that area, then adds the selected waste allowance.

The example deliberately uses the brick face plus joint rather than brick dimensions alone, because real masonry includes mortar between units.

Why mortar joint thickness matters

Bricks are not laid edge-to-edge. Horizontal bed joints and vertical perp joints increase the effective module size of each unit. A thicker joint means each brick occupies slightly more wall area, which lowers the theoretical brick count. A thinner joint does the opposite.

Use the joint size specified for the project where possible. If you are estimating an existing wall, measure several joints and use a representative average rather than relying on one unusually thick or thin joint.

Single-leaf, cavity and solid walls

The calculator treats the wall as one brick face thick for quantity purposes. A cavity wall often has an outer brick leaf and a separate inner leaf made from block or another material. A solid brick wall may use more than one leaf or header bricks tying the thickness together.

If the wall has two equivalent brick leaves, a simple starting point is to calculate each leaf separately. However, complex bonds and structural details can change the relationship, so construction drawings should take priority.

Openings for doors and windows

Large openings can be subtracted from gross wall area before estimating brick quantity. For example, a wide garage opening removes a significant amount of brickwork. Small openings do not always reduce the order by their full face area because jambs, sills, returns and cut bricks still consume material.

For a quick early estimate, leaving small openings in the gross area can function as part of the practical allowance. For a large commercial wall with repeated windows, measuring openings separately is more worthwhile.

Bond patterns and special bricks

Running bond, English bond, Flemish bond and decorative bonds arrange headers and stretchers differently. The visible wall area may be the same, but the mix of full bricks, cut bricks and specials can change. Corners, soldier courses, arches and piers also affect ordering.

The calculator is therefore best for estimating standard wall field bricks. Special shapes and feature courses should be scheduled separately if they are a meaningful part of the project.

How much waste should I add?

Waste covers breakage, cuts, damaged units and pieces that cannot be reused. A simple straight wall built with a common brick may need only a modest allowance. Complicated bonds, many corners or unusually brittle materials can justify more.

Five percent is the default here because it provides a useful starting point without assuming a large surplus. Supplier return policies and whether you want spare bricks for future repairs can influence the final order.

Brick dimensions vary by region

There is no single worldwide standard brick size. Metric bricks, modular bricks, engineering bricks and handmade products can have different face dimensions. That is why the calculator allows you to enter the actual brick face length and height instead of locking the page to one national standard.

Measure the product or use the manufacturer data sheet. When specifications list a “nominal” size that already includes a mortar joint, avoid adding the joint twice.

Does this calculator estimate mortar?

No. Mortar quantity depends on brick dimensions, joint thickness, wall thickness, frog or perforation, workmanship and waste. The joint field on this page is only used to calculate the brick module. Mortar should be estimated separately using the chosen mix and masonry specification.

Frequently asked questions

Can I calculate blocks instead of bricks?

You can enter block face dimensions for a rough unit count, but a dedicated block calculator would better reflect blockwork conventions.

Do I subtract windows and doors?

Subtract large openings when they materially reduce wall area. Small openings can sometimes be left in as part of the cutting allowance.

Does the calculator include mortar?

No. It estimates brick count only.

Can I use imperial measurements?

Yes. Imperial mode uses feet for wall dimensions and inches for brick and joint dimensions.

What if the wall is two bricks thick?

The simple face-area estimate is not enough for a thick bonded wall. Calculate each leaf only as a rough starting point and follow the wall design.

Should I keep spare bricks?

Keeping a few can be useful for future repairs, especially when colour and texture vary between production batches.
