How much mulch do I need?

Mulch is usually sold by volume rather than weight. Measure the length and width of the bed, then decide how deep the finished layer should be. The calculator multiplies those values to estimate cubic metres or cubic yards and converts the result into bags using the package size you enter.

This makes the tool useful for bark mulch, wood chips and similar loose landscape coverings where weight can vary widely between materials and moisture levels.

Choosing mulch depth

The correct depth depends on whether you are refreshing an existing bed or covering bare soil. A light top-up may only need a thin layer, while a new planting area can need substantially more. Very deep mulch can hold moisture against stems and trunks, so quantity should follow good horticultural practice rather than the idea that more is always better.

Mulch calculation example

A bed measuring 6 m × 2 m with a 7 cm mulch layer needs about 0.84 m³. That equals roughly 840 litres, or 17 bags when using 50-litre bags.

If the same bed only needed a 3 cm refresh layer, the quantity would be much lower. Depth is therefore one of the most important inputs in the calculation.

Bagged mulch or bulk mulch?

Bagged mulch is easy to transport in small quantities, keeps the material contained and can be convenient where access is narrow. Bulk mulch is often more practical for large landscape areas because dozens of individual bags become time-consuming to move and open.

Use the cubic-volume result when requesting a bulk delivery quote. Use the bag count when comparing packaged products. Since bag sizes vary by brand and country, enter the exact litre size printed on the bag.

Why mulch settles and appears to shrink

Fresh mulch contains many air gaps. Rain, irrigation and foot traffic help pieces settle into a denser layer. Organic mulches also break down gradually, which means the visible depth can reduce over time even when no material has been removed.

This settling is normal and is one reason established beds periodically need topping up. For a new installation, spread the material evenly and check the actual finished depth rather than relying only on how high the pile looked before spreading.

Measuring curved and irregular beds

Curved borders can make area estimation awkward. A practical approach is to divide the bed into several simple rectangles and calculate each section separately. For a long curved strip, measure the average width at several points and multiply that by the total length. The more irregular the shape, the more useful several smaller measurements become.

Avoid measuring the largest surrounding rectangle if much of that rectangle is actually lawn, paving or another area that will not receive mulch. That can overstate the quantity considerably.

Mulch around trees and shrubs

When mulching around established plants, the total area is not the only consideration. Mulch should generally be kept away from direct contact with trunks and stems. If a bed contains many large plants, the true covered area may be slightly smaller than the full rectangle, but it is often simpler to leave that small difference as part of the practical allowance.

Organic versus decorative mulch

Bark, shredded wood and wood chips gradually decompose and become part of the soil. Decorative mineral products behave differently and are better estimated with a stone or gravel calculator because their weight and long-term settling characteristics are different. This page is aimed at lightweight landscape mulch sold mainly by volume.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for bark chips?

Yes. The volume calculation works for bark, wood chips and similar loose landscape coverings.

Why does fresh mulch seem to shrink?

Pieces settle into gaps and organic material breaks down over time, so a layer can become thinner after installation.

Can I calculate several beds?

Yes. Calculate each bed separately and add the volumes, especially if they have different depths.

Does the calculator estimate weight?

No. Mulch density varies too much between bark, wood chips, moisture levels and products, so volume and bag count are more useful.

What if my bags are not 50 litres?

Change the bag-size field to the exact package volume and the calculator will round up to the required number of bags.

Can I use imperial measurements?

Yes. Switch to Imperial to enter feet or inches and view the result in cubic yards and cubic feet.
