How many pavers do I need?

The calculation compares the total project area with the face area of one paver. For a simple rectangular layout, divide project area by paver area, then add a waste allowance and round up to a whole unit.

The waste percentage matters because edge cuts, breakage and pattern layout can all increase the number of pavers required.

Paver size and laying patterns

A running bond or straight grid can create different cutting waste from a diagonal or complex pattern. This calculator does not model individual cuts; instead, it lets you choose an allowance that reflects the layout.

If you are using a mixed-size paving system sold by square metre or square foot rather than individual pieces, calculate the overall area and follow the manufacturer's pack coverage instead.

Paver calculation example

A 5 m × 4 m patio covers 20 m². A 20 cm × 10 cm paver covers 0.02 m², so the theoretical count is 1,000 pavers. With 10% waste, the estimate becomes 1,100 pavers.

Remember the bedding layer

The paver count only covers the paving units themselves. Most projects also need a prepared base, bedding sand and jointing material. Those layers should be calculated separately because their depth and material type depend on the installation.

Frequently asked questions

How much waste should I allow?

Simple rectangular layouts often need less waste than diagonal patterns or areas with many curves and edges. Ten percent is a common planning allowance, not a rule.

Does mortar joint width affect the count?

Yes. Wide joints slightly reduce the number of units. This calculator treats the entered paver dimensions as the effective module size, so include joint spacing if it is significant.

Can I calculate imperial pavers?

Yes. Switch to Imperial: area dimensions are entered in feet and paver dimensions in inches.
