How many drywall sheets do I need?

Multiply total wall length by wall height to find gross wall area. Then divide that area by the face area of one sheet. The calculator adds a waste allowance and rounds the result up to a whole board.

This is most useful for early material planning. Actual board layout should consider stud spacing, joints, openings and whether boards are installed horizontally or vertically.

Sheet size affects waste

Large sheets can cover more wall with fewer joints, but they can be harder to carry and may create unusable offcuts in small rooms. Standard board sizes also vary by country and supplier, so the sheet fields are editable rather than fixed.

Drywall calculation example

A wall run totaling 12 m at 2.5 m high has 30 m² of gross area. A 1.2 m × 2.4 m sheet covers 2.88 m². With 10% waste, the estimate is 12 sheets.

Doors, windows and multiple layers

For quick estimating, many small openings can be left in the gross area because the offcuts around them may be useful elsewhere. Large doors or windows can be subtracted manually if they significantly reduce board area.

Some fire, acoustic or impact-rated systems use two layers of board. If your specification calls for multiple layers, multiply the sheet result by the number of layers required.

Frequently asked questions

Is drywall the same as plasterboard?

The terms are commonly used for gypsum board products, although product names and specifications vary by region.

Does this include ceiling boards?

No. Calculate the ceiling area separately using room length × width and add the required sheet quantity.

Can I use 4 × 8 ft sheets?

Yes. Switch to Imperial and enter sheet width 4 ft and length 8 ft.
