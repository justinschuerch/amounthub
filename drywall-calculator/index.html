How many drywall sheets do I need?

Multiply total wall length by wall height to find gross wall area. Divide that area by the face area of one sheet, then add a waste allowance and round up to a complete board. The calculator is designed for early material planning, while the actual board layout should still account for framing, joints, openings and sheet orientation.

Because board dimensions vary by region and supplier, the sheet width and length fields are editable instead of being locked to one standard size.

Drywall calculation example

A wall run totaling 12 m at 2.5 m high has 30 m² of gross area. A 1.2 m × 2.4 m sheet covers 2.88 m². The theoretical minimum is just over 10 sheets, and with 10% waste the estimate rounds to 12 sheets.

Theoretical area alone does not guarantee an efficient layout. A wall can sometimes need an extra sheet because the remaining offcuts do not fit the next opening or row.

Sheet orientation can change practical waste

Drywall can be installed horizontally or vertically depending on the wall system, framing and local practice. The same total wall area may produce different offcuts with each orientation. Long boards can reduce the number of butt joints, but they may be difficult to move through stairways or small rooms.

The calculator estimates by area rather than simulating a full board layout. For a large project, sketching the sheet layout can reveal whether an alternative sheet length reduces waste.

Doors and windows

Gross wall area includes openings. For a quick estimate, small doors and windows are sometimes left in the wall area because the offcuts created around them are not always reusable elsewhere. Large openings can be subtracted if they significantly reduce the board area.

If a room has a full-width opening, large glazing or multiple wide doors, subtracting the actual opening area can noticeably improve the estimate. For a normal door in an otherwise full wall, leaving it in the gross area can act as part of the waste allowance.

Do I need to calculate both sides of a partition?

Yes. A freestanding partition normally has board on both faces. If the wall length and height describe only one face, multiply the resulting wall area by two before comparing it with sheet area, or run the calculator twice. If the specification calls for two layers on each face, the quantity increases again.

Fire-rated and acoustic assemblies

Some wall systems use multiple layers of gypsum board, special board types or staggered joints. The calculator does not design those assemblies. If the approved wall specification requires two layers, multiply the single-layer sheet count by the number of layers, then consider whether the waste pattern is the same for each layer.

Fire, moisture and acoustic ratings depend on the complete tested system, not merely the number of sheets.

Ceiling drywall

Ceilings are best calculated separately because their area is based on room length × width, not wall perimeter × height. Measure the ceiling area, divide by the chosen sheet area and add an allowance. Ceiling work may also use different board thicknesses or support spacing, so keep that quantity separate from wall sheets if the products differ.

Waste, damage and handling

Drywall edges and corners are vulnerable during transport. Cut-outs for outlets, pipes and doors can also create pieces that are too small to reuse. Ten percent is a convenient default for planning, but a simple open wall may use less and a renovation with many obstacles may use more.

Ordering one extra board can sometimes be cheaper than stopping work to collect a single replacement sheet, but large projects should be planned more carefully to avoid unnecessary surplus.

What else is needed besides sheets?

A drywall project may require screws or nails, joint tape, joint compound, corner bead, sealant and possibly insulation or resilient channels. Those products are not included in the sheet count because they use different estimating methods. The Paint Calculator can help estimate finish paint after the wall is prepared and primed.

Frequently asked questions

Is drywall the same as plasterboard?

The names are commonly used for gypsum board products, although product standards and brand terminology vary by region.

Does this include ceiling boards?

No. Calculate ceiling area separately using room length × width.

Can I use 4 × 8 ft sheets?

Yes. Switch to Imperial and enter sheet width 4 ft and length 8 ft.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Subtract large openings if they materially reduce area. Leaving small openings in the gross area can help cover cutting waste.

How do I calculate a two-sided partition?

Count both wall faces. Double the one-face area or calculate each side separately.

Does the calculator account for multiple layers?

No. Multiply the sheet requirement by the number of layers specified for the assembly.
