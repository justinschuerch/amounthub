How many tiles do I need?

To estimate tile quantity, first calculate the surface area, then divide by the face area of one tile. The calculator adds a waste allowance for cuts, breakage and pattern layout before rounding up to a whole tile. If the product is sold in boxes, the tile count is divided by the number of tiles per box and rounded up again.

This approach works for floors and walls as long as the surface and tile dimensions are measured consistently.

Tile calculation example

A 4 m × 3 m floor covers 12 m². A 60 cm × 30 cm tile covers 0.18 m². The theoretical count is 67 tiles; with 10% waste the estimate becomes 74 tiles. If there are six tiles per box, you would need 13 boxes.

Ten percent is a common planning figure, but the right allowance depends on the room, tile size and pattern.

Grout joints and effective tile size

Tiles are installed with grout joints, so the finished module is slightly larger than the tile itself. For many standard floor projects, the joint difference is small compared with the waste allowance. For mosaics, small tiles or unusually wide joints, it can have a more noticeable effect.

If you want the calculation to reflect joint spacing more closely, enter the tile dimension plus one joint width as the effective module size. For example, a 300 mm tile with a 3 mm joint can be treated as roughly 303 mm for layout calculations.

Floor tiles versus wall tiles

The area formula is the same, but the installation details are different. For a wall, enter wall width and height as the two surface dimensions. If the wall includes a large door, window, shower niche or other opening, subtracting that area can improve the estimate. Small openings are often left in the gross area because cuts around them still consume tile.

Measuring irregular rooms

Split L-shaped floors into rectangles and add the areas. For bathrooms with alcoves or built-in cabinetry, calculate each exposed floor section separately. The same method works for wall tiling: divide a room into individual wall rectangles and add only the surfaces that will actually be tiled.

This is more accurate than measuring one large bounding rectangle that includes areas under permanent fixtures or outside the tiled zone.

Tiles sold by box coverage instead of tile count

Some manufacturers list square metres or square feet per box rather than emphasizing the number of pieces. In that case, use your waste-adjusted surface area and divide it by the pack coverage printed on the box. The box result from this calculator is most useful when the package states a fixed tile count.

Keeping spare tiles for future repairs

Saving a few unused tiles can be valuable. Colours, surface texture and dimensions can change between production batches, and a matching replacement may be difficult to find years later. If the job uses a distinctive tile, consider whether your waste allowance should include a small reserve after installation rather than only installation waste.

What the calculator does not include

The tile count does not estimate adhesive, mortar, grout, backer board, waterproofing or trim profiles. Those products are normally measured using different coverage rules. It also does not decide whether the substrate is suitable, flat or strong enough for the chosen tile.

Choosing tile orientation

Turning a rectangular tile 90 degrees does not change its area, so the theoretical count stays the same. However, orientation can change where cuts fall and therefore change practical waste. Before ordering expensive tile, sketching or dry-laying a few rows can reveal whether one orientation uses full tiles more efficiently at the most visible edges.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for wall tile?

Yes. Enter the wall width and height as the surface dimensions.

How many boxes should I buy?

The calculator rounds the waste-adjusted tile count up to full boxes using the tiles-per-box value you enter.

Does it support inches?

Yes. Imperial mode uses feet for the surface and inches for individual tile dimensions.

Should I subtract a bathtub or cabinet area?

Subtract areas that definitely will not receive tile if they are large enough to materially affect the result.

Is 10% waste enough for diagonal tile?

Not always. Diagonal or intricate patterns can create more offcuts, so a higher allowance may be sensible.

Does grout joint width matter?

Yes, especially for small tiles or wide joints. Use the tile plus joint as the effective module if you need more precision.
