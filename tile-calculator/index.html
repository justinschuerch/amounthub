How many tiles do I need?

Divide the surface area by the face area of one tile to estimate the theoretical tile count. Because tiles are cut around walls, corners, pipes and fixtures, the calculator then adds your chosen waste allowance and rounds up to a whole tile.

If tiles are sold in boxes, the final quantity is divided by the number of tiles per box and rounded up again.

Why tile waste matters

A square room with a simple straight layout can use material efficiently. Diagonal patterns, herringbone layouts, niches and irregular room shapes create more offcuts. Breakage during transport or installation can also increase demand.

Keeping a few spare tiles after the job can be useful for future repairs, especially when a colour or production batch may later be unavailable.

Tile calculation example

A 4 m × 3 m floor covers 12 m². A 60 cm × 30 cm tile covers 0.18 m². The theoretical count is 67 tiles; with 10% waste the estimate becomes 74 tiles.

What about grout joints?

Grout joints slightly increase the module size of each tile. For many projects the difference is small compared with the waste allowance, but very small tiles or wide joints can make it more noticeable. For precision layouts, use the tile plus joint dimensions as the effective size.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this for wall tile?

Yes. Enter the wall length and height as the surface dimensions.

How many boxes should I buy?

The calculator rounds the waste-adjusted tile count up to full boxes using the tiles-per-box value you enter.

Does it support inches?

Yes. Imperial mode uses feet for the surface and inches for individual tile dimensions.
