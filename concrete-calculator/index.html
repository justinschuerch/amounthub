How much concrete do I need?

The amount of concrete you need is determined by the volume of the space you plan to fill. For a rectangular slab, that means measuring the length, width and thickness, then multiplying those values together.

AmountHub's concrete calculator converts those measurements into cubic metres for metric projects or cubic yards for imperial projects. You can also add an extra allowance to cover minor measurement errors, uneven sub-base levels, spills and waste during placing.

Because concrete is usually ordered by volume rather than by weight, the calculator focuses on the final quantity of concrete to order rather than estimating tonnes of material.

How the concrete calculator works

For a rectangular slab, the core formula is:

Length × Width × Thickness = Concrete Volume

The calculator first converts all dimensions to a common unit. It then calculates the base slab volume and applies the extra allowance percentage you choose.

For example, a 10% allowance multiplies the base volume by 1.10. This gives a practical order estimate while still showing the unadjusted base volume separately in the result.

Concrete calculation example

Suppose you are pouring a slab that is 5 metres long, 3 metres wide and 10 centimetres thick.

The slab area is 15 m². A thickness of 10 cm is 0.10 m, so the base concrete volume is:

5 × 3 × 0.10 = 1.50 m³

With a 10% extra allowance, the estimated order quantity becomes approximately 1.65 m³.

Common concrete slab thicknesses

The correct slab thickness depends on how the slab will be used, the ground conditions and the structural design. The figures below are general planning examples rather than engineering recommendations.

Project typeTypical planning range
Garden path or light walkwayAbout 75–100 mm
Patio or small slabAbout 100 mm
Shed baseAbout 100–125 mm
Driveway or vehicle slabOften 125 mm or more

Heavier loads, weak ground, reinforcement requirements and local building standards can all affect the required slab design. For structural work, follow the project specification or seek professional advice.

Should I order extra concrete?

Ordering exactly the theoretical volume can leave very little room for variation. Excavations may be slightly deeper than planned, timber formwork may not be perfectly straight and some concrete can be lost during transport, pumping or placement.

A 5–10% allowance is often used for simple planning estimates. The calculator starts at 10%, but you can change the percentage to match the accuracy of your measurements and the conditions on site.

For a large concrete delivery, it is sensible to confirm the final order quantity with the concrete supplier or contractor before ordering.

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Bagged concrete is normally sold by bag weight, but the actual finished volume depends on the product and mix. A 20 kg, 25 kg, 40 lb or 80 lb bag will not all produce the same volume.

The most reliable method is to use this calculator to find the total concrete volume first, then check the yield printed on the bag or the manufacturer's product data. Divide the required volume by the stated yield per bag to estimate how many bags you need.

If the packaging says one bag produces 0.01 m³ and your project requires 0.50 m³, for example, you would need about 50 bags before adding any extra allowance.

Cubic metres and cubic yards of concrete

Ready-mix concrete is commonly quoted in cubic metres in metric countries and cubic yards in the United States. These units measure volume, not area.

One cubic metre is roughly 1.31 cubic yards. The calculator handles this conversion automatically when you switch between Metric and Imperial, so you do not need to convert the order quantity manually.

Thickness matters greatly because even a small change in slab depth affects the total volume across the whole area.

Can I use this calculator for patios, paths and shed bases?

Yes. The calculator works well for simple rectangular pours such as patios, shed bases, garden paths, garage slabs and other flat rectangular areas.

If your project contains several separate rectangles, calculate each section individually and add the volumes together. For irregular shapes, divide the area into simpler sections where possible.

Projects involving footings, columns, circular slabs, steps or complex foundations need different formulas, so this calculator should not be treated as a structural design tool.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator support feet and inches?

Yes. Switch to Imperial to enter measurements in feet or inches. The result is shown in cubic yards and cubic feet.

Can I estimate concrete cost?

Yes. Enter a price per cubic metre or cubic yard in the optional price field and the calculator will estimate the material cost from the final volume.

Is the 10% allowance always necessary?

No. It is a general planning allowance. You can reduce, increase or remove it depending on how precise your measurements are and how much variation you expect.

Does this calculate reinforcement or rebar?

No. It estimates concrete volume only. Reinforcement requirements depend on the structural design and should be calculated separately.

Can I use this for ready-mix concrete?

Yes. The volume result is suitable for estimating a ready-mix order, but you should confirm the final order quantity with your supplier before delivery.
