Why these calculators are different

AmountHub does not use one formula with different labels. Loose materials such as gravel, sand and soil need volume and density assumptions. Concrete is ordered by volume. Pavers, tiles, bricks and drywall depend on the size of individual units. Paint depends on coverage and coats, while flooring depends on pack coverage. Each calculator is built around the purchasing decision a visitor is actually trying to make.

Transparent assumptions

Where a material can vary, the calculator explains the assumption instead of presenting a planning value as an exact specification. Density, coverage, waste, pack sizes and installation conditions can differ by supplier and product. When a manufacturer or supplier gives a product-specific figure, that figure should take priority.

How results are checked

Calculator pages include the underlying formula, a worked example and notes about rounding or extra-material allowances. Inputs are validated so that impossible values do not produce a plausible-looking result. Read the full calculator methodology and quality standards for more detail.

Planning estimates, not project design

These tools are intended for quantity planning and shopping preparation. They do not replace structural calculations, code requirements, supplier specifications or professional site advice. For regulated or safety-critical work, verify the result with the appropriate professional or manufacturer.
