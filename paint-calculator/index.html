How much paint do I need?

Paint quantity depends on the area to be coated, the number of coats and the coverage rate printed on the paint container. This calculator uses room perimeter multiplied by wall height to estimate total wall area, then subtracts standard allowances for doors and windows.

The remaining wall area is multiplied by the number of coats and divided by the coverage rate.

Why paint coverage varies

Smooth sealed plaster can use less paint than rough masonry or highly absorbent drywall. A strong colour change can also require extra coats. Manufacturer coverage figures are useful starting points, but real-world use can differ.

Paint calculation example

A room with an 18 m perimeter and 2.5 m walls has 45 m² of gross wall area. After allowing for one door and two windows, two coats at 10 m²/L require roughly 8 litres of paint.

Should ceilings be included?

This calculator is designed for walls. If you are painting a ceiling, calculate its length × width separately and add that area to the coated area, or run a separate calculation using the product's ceiling-paint coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Does primer count as a paint coat?

Primer is usually a separate product with its own coverage rate, so calculate it separately rather than adding it to the finish-coat count.

Why subtract doors and windows?

They reduce the wall surface being painted. For unusual opening sizes, the standard allowances here may be less accurate.

Can I use gallons?

Yes. Imperial mode converts the result to US gallons and uses square feet per gallon for coverage.
