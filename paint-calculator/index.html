How much paint do I need?

Paint quantity depends on the area being coated, the number of coats and the coverage rate of the specific product. This calculator estimates gross wall area from room perimeter multiplied by wall height, subtracts standard allowances for doors and windows, multiplies the remaining area by the number of coats, and divides by the coverage rate.

The result is an estimate in litres or US gallons. It is most useful when you enter the coverage figure printed on the can rather than relying only on the default.

Paint calculation example

A room with an 18 m perimeter and 2.5 m walls has 45 m² of gross wall area. After allowing for one door and two windows, the paintable area is about 40.1 m². Two coats at 10 m²/L require roughly 8 litres of paint.

If the same paint only covered 8 m² per litre on a rough wall, the required quantity would rise. Coverage rate is therefore just as important as room size.

How many coats should I plan for?

Two finish coats are common for many interior projects because they improve colour consistency and durability, but there is no universal number. A repaint in a similar colour may cover quickly, while a dramatic colour change or an uneven substrate can need more work. Premium high-opacity paints can sometimes achieve good coverage with fewer coats than cheaper or more translucent products.

Use the coat field to model different scenarios before buying. For example, compare two coats with three coats and see how much extra paint the third coat requires.

Primer is usually a separate calculation

Primer should not normally be treated as just another coat of finish paint because it is a different product with its own coverage rate. If the project needs primer, calculate the wall area once using the primer manufacturer's coverage, then calculate the finish coats separately.

This matters on bare drywall, repaired areas, stained surfaces, glossy finishes and strong colour changes where the correct primer can improve adhesion and reduce finish-paint consumption.

Doors and windows

The calculator uses standard allowances for openings so you do not have to measure every door and window. That keeps the tool quick, but unusual rooms can benefit from manual measurement. A wall of large glazing may have much less paintable area than the standard window allowance assumes, while tiny windows may barely affect the total.

If you need a more precise estimate, calculate gross wall area and subtract the actual opening areas separately before dividing by coverage.

What about ceilings?

This calculator focuses on walls. A ceiling is usually easier to calculate from room length × room width. Multiply the ceiling area by the number of coats and divide by the ceiling paint's coverage. Keep ceiling paint separate if it has a different sheen, colour or coverage rate from the wall paint.

Buying paint in real container sizes

Paint is sold in fixed tins or buckets, not exact decimal quantities. If the calculator says 8.1 litres, you may need to buy the next combination of available container sizes. It can be useful to leave a small amount for touch-ups after furniture is moved back, fixtures are installed or the room is used.

When colour consistency matters, buying enough material from the same tint batch can be preferable to returning later for a small extra container.

Measuring several rooms

For multiple rooms, calculate each one separately if they use different colours or finishes. If all rooms use the same paint, add their paintable wall areas before applying the coat count. Hallways, stairwells and rooms with many openings can be easier to estimate as separate zones.

Interior versus exterior paint estimates

The same basic area-and-coverage formula applies outside, but exterior walls often have rougher textures, weathered surfaces, more preparation and different coatings. Siding profiles can also create more actual surface area than a flat wall measurement suggests. For exterior work, treat the calculator as a starting estimate and use the manufacturer's exterior coverage guidance.

Frequently asked questions

Does primer count as a paint coat?

Primer is usually a separate product with its own coverage rate, so calculate it separately.

Why subtract doors and windows?

They reduce the wall surface being painted. For unusual opening sizes, standard allowances may be less accurate.

Can I use gallons?

Yes. Imperial mode uses square feet per US gallon and returns the quantity in US gallons.

Should I buy exactly the calculator result?

Usually you will need to round up to available tin sizes and may want a small amount left for touch-ups.

Why did my real paint use exceed the estimate?

Porous walls, texture, repairs, colour changes, application losses and lower-than-advertised real-world coverage can all increase consumption.

Can I calculate one feature wall?

Yes. Use the length of that wall as the perimeter input and its height, then set doors and windows as appropriate.
